# Explanations

* [Why a term is a record](terms-as-records.md) - Why D3FEND's terms are records and not LinkML classes: D3FEND's own conventions pun its techniques as instances, its distribution states nearly every class restriction again as a direct triple between terms, and most of its 4,465 classes are not types anyone instantiates.
* [How the kinds are chosen](kinds-of-entry.md) - How a term gets its kind: the first in the converter's table whose branch it sits under, the deeper before its parent and D3FEND's own branches before the external ones, so that a term with two parents has one kind.
* [What a slot may hold](what-a-slot-may-hold.md) - Which kinds hold which slots, and why most may hold any: the ontology states a domain for few of its properties, so no domain is invented. A literal slot holds one value where no term of this edition holds two.
* [Mappings](mappings.md) - What the schema maps to and how each mapping is held: lmodel/attack, stix, cwe, capec and nist-sp-800-53 by their released copies, and SKOS, IAO, BFO and the Common Core Ontologies because the ontology itself names their terms. The earlier guessed mappings are gone.
* [RDF from the catalogue](rdf.md) - How the catalogue becomes RDF: every identifier is a CURIE, so both the JSON-LD context and LinkML's own dumper give a record's IRI and its relations as D3FEND's own. `rdfs:seeAlso` and `rdfs:isDefinedBy` project as text.
* [What the schema does not cover](scope.md) - What the schema leaves out on purpose: D3FEND's classes as LinkML classes, the 340 abstracts of cited sources, the restrictions the edition does not obey, two leftover subjects of WebProtege, the SQL form, and any claim about whether what an entry says is true.
* [What the data shows](what-the-data-shows.md) - What the catalogue holds as the file holds it, faults included: a CAPEC id that names another entry, 27 duplicated D3FEND ids, three empty links, and terms with two labels or three definitions. A test records each, so a later edition that fixes one fails.
